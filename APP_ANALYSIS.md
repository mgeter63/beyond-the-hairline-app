# Beyond the Hairline: Product Overview and Marketing Reference

Two products from Beyond the Hairline, described for anyone who needs to explain them, write about them, or market them accurately.

- **I Have This Too**, the scarring alopecia patient registry. A one-time intake.
- **Track It**, the free 30-day scalp journal. Given to every registry participant.

This document describes what each product does and what it asks of the person using it. It contains no technical, security, or account detail by design. Marketing copy in Section E may be used as written.

---

## A. What each product is

### I Have This Too

A patient registry for scarring alopecia, including CCCA (Central Centrifugal Cicatricial Alopecia), LPP, FFA, DLE, folliculitis decalvans, acne keloidalis nuchae, and dissecting cellulitis. A person creates an account, reads and agrees to a consent, and completes one guided intake in four short steps. That single entry is the product. It is not a daily tracker and there is nothing to keep up with afterward.

The registry is maintained by Beyond the Hairline LLC in partnership with Chat Chew and Do Empowerment Women Group (CCDEW), a Mississippi nonprofit. Its purpose is to document, in one place, who scarring alopecia affects, how long diagnosis takes, how it shapes daily life, and what stands between people and care.

Beyond the Hairline began as a tool for women living with CCCA. It has grown into a registry for everyone scarring alopecia touches: all genders, all races and ethnicities, and all seven conditions listed above. The intake reflects that. Gender and identity questions offer a full range of answers, and the hair care and product language adapts to the person answering.

### Track It

A free, private 30-day scalp journal. A person rates ten common scalp and hair symptoms each day, taps what they did with their hair, adds a note, and saves. Everything stays in the browser on their own device. At any point they can print the journal or download it to share with a dermatologist.

Track It is offered to people who complete the registry. It is a project of Beyond the Hairline and CCDEW.

## B. What each product asks for

### B1. I Have This Too

**Creating an account**
- The name you want to be called
- Email address
- Mobile phone number
- A password
- Two confirmations: that you agree to the Privacy Policy and Terms of Service, and that you are 18 or older

**Consent**
Before any question is asked, the participant reads a consent explaining that de-identified answers may be shared with the registry's partners and with research and pharmaceutical organizations to advance scarring alopecia research, and agrees or declines. Declining ends the visit. The agreement is recorded with its date and version.

**Step 1, Who You Are**
- Race and ethnicity (select all that apply, including "I prefer not to say")
- Gender (including "I prefer not to say")
- Five-digit zip code

**Step 2, Your Condition**
- Whether you have a formal diagnosis, are pursuing one, or are self-researching
- Which condition, if diagnosed
- When symptoms first began
- How often symptoms occur
- Whether symptoms are getting better, staying the same, or getting worse
- Your natural hair texture

**Step 3, Your Symptoms**
- Roughly what percentage of your scalp is affected
- Where on your scalp or hairline
- How each of sixteen common symptoms feels right now, from none to extreme: burning, itching, tingling or crawling, scalp pain, redness, tenderness, bumps, pustules, blisters, scaling or flaking, scabs or crusting, oozing or draining, increased shedding, widening part, crown thinning, and a new or expanding bald spot
- Thirteen optional questions about daily life: how you feel about your hair, self-esteem, avoiding social situations, emotional wellbeing, sleep, work and daily activities, whether your provider understands your condition, confidence in your appearance, how you dress, exercise, worry, intimate relationships, and the burden of managing treatment

**Step 4, Your Care Journey** (all optional)
- How long from first noticing hair loss to diagnosis or treatment
- How many providers you saw before diagnosis or treatment
- Whether cost ever prevented you from seeking care
- Whether your provider understands scarring alopecia as it affects people of your background

**Completion**
A thank-you screen with a link to share the registry. If the daily-life answers suggest the condition is weighing heavily, the screen also offers a mental health referral and the 988 crisis line. The participant then receives Track It.

**What the registry does not ask for:** photos, daily symptom logs, hair care routines, appointment notes, purchase costs, a legal name, a home address, or payment details.

### B2. Track It

**To receive the journal**
- First name
- Email address
- An optional checkbox to hear about updates and future studies. Receiving the journal does not depend on it.

**Each day**
- Ten symptoms rated from none to severe: itching, burning or stinging, tenderness or soreness, tightness or pulling, flaking or scaling, redness, increased shedding, crown thinning, hairline recession, and other scalp change
- What you did with your hair: style or tension, heat used, products on the scalp, chemicals or treatments
- A note for the day

**What Track It does not do:** it does not upload entries, take photos, send reminders, or require a password. Signing out deletes the journal from the device, so the person is encouraged to print or download first.

## C. How each product is used, start to finish

### C1. I Have This Too

1. Arrive at the registry, often through a stylist's or barber's QR code, a partner link, or the Beyond the Hairline website.
2. Create an account and confirm your email.
3. Read the consent and agree.
4. Complete Steps 1 through 4. Progress is saved, so a person can stop and return.
5. See the thank-you screen, share the registry if you wish, and finish.
6. Receive Track It.

Signing in again later shows a simple "your registration is complete" screen. There is nothing further to do.

### C2. Track It

1. Open the journal and enter a first name and email.
2. Rate today's symptoms, tap today's hair practices, add a note, save.
3. Come back tomorrow. A greeting bar welcomes you by name.
4. When you are ready, print or download the journal for your dermatologist.

### C3. How professionals feed the registry

Cosmetologists, barbers, and instructors who complete the Beyond the Hairline continuing education course receive a personal referral card with a QR code. When a client scans it and completes the registry, the registry notes which professional referred them. The Beyond the Hairline website tells the story of a barber who noticed thinning at a client's crown, handed him a code, and the client "had Track It before I went to sleep."

## D. Privacy and consent, in plain terms

- **Consent is a real step, not a link.** No registry question is asked until the participant has read the consent and agreed, and the agreement is recorded.
- **Age and terms are confirmed at sign-up.** Both are required checkboxes.
- **"Prefer not to say" is always available** for race, ethnicity, and gender.
- **The registry is one entry, not surveillance.** There is no ongoing logging, no photo upload, and no location tracking beyond a zip code.
- **Track It never leaves the device.** Journal entries are not uploaded anywhere. Only the first name and email given to receive the journal are sent, and the person chooses separately whether to be contacted.
- **Neither product is a medical service.** Both state plainly that they do not diagnose, treat, or advise, and that a board-certified dermatologist should be consulted.
- **Data sharing is de-identified.** The consent and both privacy policies state that individually identifiable data is not sold and that research sharing is in de-identified form.
- **Both products have a published Privacy Policy and Terms of Service** and are intended for adults 18 and older.

Questions about privacy, consent, or data use should go to the Beyond the Hairline contact listed on the website.

## E. Marketing materials

The offer, as designed: **complete the registry once, receive Track It free.**

### E1. I Have This Too (patient registry)

**One-line tagline**
Your story counts. Add it to the record.

**What it is (3 to 5 words)**
The scarring alopecia patient registry.

**Three core benefits**
1. Be counted. One form, about ten minutes, and your experience becomes part of a national picture of who scarring alopecia affects, how long diagnosis takes, and what care really looks like.
2. Get Track It free. Registry participants receive the private 30-day scalp journal, which is not available anywhere else.
3. Be seen as a whole person. The registry asks how the condition affects your sleep, your confidence, your relationships, and your worry, and if your answers show the weight is heavy, it points you to a mental health provider who understands hair loss.

**What it captures, in patient-friendly words**
You create an account with your email, a mobile number, and the name you want to be called. You read and agree to how your de-identified answers may be used. Then four short steps: who you are, your condition, your symptoms and how they affect daily life, and your care journey. That is it. No photos, no daily logging, no ongoing commitment.

**Who it is for and the one problem it solves**
Adults of every race and gender who are living with, being evaluated for, or quietly researching scarring alopecia. That includes the Black women and men CCCA affects most, and everyone this condition has left uncounted. The problem: this condition has no national dataset. Nobody can say how many people have it, how long they wait, or what it costs them, so it stays under-researched and under-treated. The registry is that dataset, built one voice at a time.

**Five short social captions**
1. Scarring alopecia has no national patient registry. Until now. Add your voice in ten minutes, once, and get Track It free.
2. How long did it take you to get a diagnosis? How many doctors? Did cost ever stop you? These answers are the data that changes care. Join the registry.
3. Your stylist noticed. Your barber noticed. Now the record can too. Scan the code, complete the registry, and your story counts.
4. Sixteen symptoms. Thirteen questions about real life. One form. Join the first patient registry built for scarring alopecia.
5. Not diagnosed yet? You still belong here. The registry is for everyone who knows something is wrong with their scalp.

### E2. Track It (free 30-day scalp journal)

**One-line tagline**
Thirty days. Your scalp. Your receipts.

**What it is (3 to 5 words)**
Your private scalp journal.

**Three core benefits**
1. Two minutes a day. Rate ten symptoms, tap what you did with your hair, add a note, save.
2. Nothing leaves your phone. Entries live in your own browser. No account, no password, no cloud.
3. Proof for your dermatologist. Print it or download it and hand over a day-by-day record instead of a memory.

**What it captures, in patient-friendly words**
Each day you rate ten common scalp and hair symptoms, like itching, burning, flaking, shedding, and thinning at the crown or hairline, from none to severe. You tap the style you wore, any heat, any products on your scalp, and any chemicals. You can add a note about sleep, stress, or anything you noticed. All of it stays in your browser on your device. When you are ready, print it or download it for your doctor. Signing out wipes it clean.

**Who it is for and the one problem it solves**
Registry participants who want to keep watching their scalp after the one-time intake, and anyone who suspects something is wrong but does not yet have a diagnosis or a doctor. The problem: symptoms that go unwritten go unnoticed, and most people arrive at a dermatologist with nothing to show. Track It is the something.

### E3. How the two products work together

I Have This Too is the front door and Track It is the gift on the other side of it. A person arrives at the registry through a stylist's QR card, a partner link, or the website, creates an account, reads and agrees to the consent, and completes one ten-minute intake: who they are, their condition, their symptoms and quality of life, and their care journey. That single entry is the product. It is never repeated, it never becomes a daily chore, and it is what builds the national dataset. In return, every participant receives Track It, the free 30-day scalp journal that is not offered anywhere else. Track It picks up where the registry stops: the registry captures one snapshot, and the journal lets the person keep watching their own scalp day by day, privately, on their own phone, with a printout to bring to the dermatologist. The two are deliberately different in character. The registry is consented and shared in de-identified form for research. The journal is personal, on-device, and shared with no one unless the person prints it. Together they give a person two things scarring alopecia has denied them for too long: a place to be counted, and a record of their own.
