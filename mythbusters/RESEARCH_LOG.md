### Findings
1. Who posts alopecia content on Instagram: a 2026 JMIR Dermatology study of 90 Instagram posts on alopecia areata found 66% came from non-medical influencers, 29% from hair and wig companies, and only 4% from medical professionals. Takeaway: doctor-backed content is rare, which is our edge. Source: derma.jmir.org/2026/1/e78262
2. Dermatologists vs influencers: a January 2026 study found similar engagement for both, but dermatologists were more likely to recommend standard treatments (49% vs 27%). Takeaway: accurate content does not have to lose on engagement. Source: pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41912215
3. Silent viewing: claims that 85% of people watch muted are widely repeated but not well sourced. The most data-based figure found (Emplifi benchmark) shows text overlays add about 5% retention at 3 seconds and 7.5% at 10 seconds. Takeaway: on-screen text helps, especially in the first seconds, but it is not magic. Our format (text first, readable muted) fits this.
4. Caption style: vendor tests (not independent) report that emphasized key words in color improve watch time. Takeaway: we highlight one key phrase per fact. Treat the size of the effect as unproven.
5. Length: one 2026 benchmark puts median Reel retention near 32%, dropping sharply after 60 seconds. Our 14-second length is well inside the safe range.
6. Posting times (general data, not health-specific):
   - Instagram Reels: Sprout Social 2026 points to weekday afternoons, about 2 to 5 pm (audience local time).
   - Facebook: Sprout 2026 says Monday to Thursday are strongest, Wednesday highest. Later says Thursday 9 am.
   - Recommendation to test: 12:30 pm Central on weekdays, then compare against a 3 pm slot after two weeks.
7. Hashtags: no reliable public ranking of alopecia hashtags by engagement was found. A 2024 study of hair-loss short videos shows broad tags like #hairloss are used far less on Instagram than condition-specific ones. We use the approved hashtag bank from the Master Log, always with #BeyondTheHairline, 8 to 12 tags per post.
8. Competitor pattern (from the Master Log snapshots, still current): oil "cure" content dominates the hair-loss hashtags. A calm, sourced voice stands out.
